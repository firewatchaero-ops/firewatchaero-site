The Plan

How FireWatch fights a fire

The full system is a chain: towers watch the horizon, a mothership drone confirms and maps the fire, and a swarm of payload gliders contains it — all coordinated automatically and visible to the agencies responding. We're building it one proven layer at a time, starting with the mothership.

Phase 1 · Building Now

Confirm — The Mothership

A tower-based mothership drone flies out and drops to a lower elevation over a suspected ignition. Its onboard thermal camera and AI confirm whether there's a fire, pinpoint its exact location, and map its edges — then relay a confirmed report to nearby crews through our monitoring software. A distant smoke sighting becomes precise, actionable coordinates in minutes.

Phase 2

Watch — Autonomous Towers

Fire watch towers — partnered state towers or our own autonomous ones — carry cameras, thermal sights, and sensors that scan the horizon for smoke and heat. When the AI believes a fire has started, it triangulates the location and dispatches the mothership to investigate on its own, no human trigger required. Each tower hosts the mothership and becomes the launch point for everything that follows.

○ Next Up
Phase 3

Contain — The Glider Swarm

Once the fire is confirmed, the mothership signals drone launchers at its tower and on surrounding mountaintops to release payload gliders. Each glider extends its wings, cruises to the fire at up to 80 mph, and uses its own thermal camera and AI to adjust course mid-flight before dropping a fire-suppressant payload. The mothership choreographs the whole swarm — flight paths, release points, and calling in more gliders when the fire demands it — holding the fire small until crews arrive with heavier resources.
