The Problem

Wildfires are won or lost in the first hour

A wildfire grows exponentially. While it's small, it's containable — but in remote terrain, both halves of the response are too slow. It takes too long to confirm a fire, and even longer for the first water or retardant to reach it. By the time planes, crews, and drones arrive, a spark has become a fire front.

Detection is too slow

Satellites pass every few hours, towers need line of sight, and crews rely on someone reporting smoke. A remote ignition can burn unnoticed for 15–30 minutes or more before anyone confirms it.

The first response is even slower

Once a fire is confirmed, water and retardant still have to travel to it. In the backcountry, aircraft, engines, and hand crews can be hours away — and every one of those minutes lets the fire grow.

Fire grows exponentially

Wildfires spread fastest in their later stages. A few extra minutes early on is the difference between a spot fire and a megafire — and between a routine knockdown and a season-defining disaster.

The Plan

How FireWatch fights a fire

The full system is a chain: towers watch the horizon, a mothership drone confirms and maps the fire, and a swarm of payload gliders contains it — all coordinated automatically and visible to the agencies responding. We're building it one proven layer at a time, starting with the mothership.

Phase 1 · Building Now

Confirm — The Mothership

A tower-based mothership drone flies to a suspected ignition, and its onboard thermal camera and AI confirm whether there's a fire, pinpoint its exact location, and map its edges. It relays a confirmed report to nearby crews through our monitoring software — turning a distant smoke sighting into precise, actionable coordinates in minutes.

● In Development
Phase 2

Watch — Autonomous Towers

Fire watch towers — partnered state towers or our own autonomous ones — carry cameras, thermal sights, and sensors that scan the horizon for smoke and heat. When the AI believes a fire has started, it dispatches the mothership to investigate on its own, no human trigger required. Each tower hosts the mothership and becomes the launch point for everything that follows.

○ Next Up
Phase 3

Contain — The Glider Swarm

Once the fire is confirmed, the mothership signals drone launchers at its tower and on surrounding mountaintops to release payload gliders. Each glider extends its wings, cruises to the fire at up to 80 mph, and uses its own thermal camera and AI to adjust course mid-flight before dropping a fire-suppressant payload. The mothership choreographs the whole swarm — flight paths, release points, and calling in more gliders when the fire demands it — holding the fire small until crews arrive with heavier resources.

How it works today

The entire pipeline is self-contained. Both devices auto-connect and start streaming on boot — no manual setup, no cloud, no internet.

01

Thermal Camera

Reads 768 temperature points 4× per second. Detects heat invisible to the human eye.

02

Drone Processor

Onboard Pi processes the stream and transmits over a private radio link.

03

HaLow Radio Link

900 MHz link carries data kilometers with no cellular coverage needed.

04

Ground Station

Second Pi hosts a live web dashboard accessible from any browser.

05

Live Alert

Operator is alerted instantly when temperature exceeds threshold.

Business Model

Built for the agencies that fight fires

FireWatch is designed for local, state, and national governments — the fire and land-management agencies responsible for protecting wildland. It isn't a consumer product, though in principle it could cover rural communities far from help. We're pre-revenue today; this is how the model is built to work.

One-Time

Setup & Installation

Standing up coverage for a region: mothership drones, tower sensors, and mountaintop launchers — either integrated into existing fire watch towers or deployed as our own autonomous ones.

Annual

System & Software

A yearly fee to keep the system maintained and the software current, including access to the monitoring platform for local and state authorities coordinating the response.

Ongoing

Payload & Fleet Resupply

The core recurring cost: refilling glider suppressant payloads and replacing the gliders staged across mountaintop launchers after each deployment, so the system is always ready for the next fire.
